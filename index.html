What these numbers mean

Yellow dot: the median player at that play time, with the middle 68% of players as the yellow bar. "Real players gained" is how fast players near the median actually improved at this stage: measured from tracked players' histories, with its range in brackets. It can differ from the curve's slope, which compares different players at each play time rather than following the same ones.

Your progress
How this is recorded

Green line: your pp against your play time. Solid with dots: recorded, one point each time your stats are pulled (daily, when they've changed), kept forever. Dotted: a one-time estimate back to your first hour, from how far ahead of the typical player real players at your level were earlier on. Dashed: rebuilt from the dates of your top 100 plays and your plays per month; that can only see scores still in your top 100, so it's a floor on your past pp and is used only where it's above the estimate. Between points it's a straight line on the chart. Ranks, "better than" and the Overwatch rank at every point use today's data, so they show where that pp would stand now, not where it stood then.

How the numbers are made

Curve: the typical pp and the bands are the real median and percentiles of players at each play time (smoothed along play time, around a quadratic in ln(hours) as a backbone). σ is measured through those same percentiles: ±1σ and ±2σ are the edges of the middle 68% and 95% of real players at your play time. "Better than" compares you with every player after adjusting each one to the trend, weighting players more the closer their play time is to yours, so the number moves smoothly as you change your hours. Rank letters are this page's own labels for that percentile, not in-game grades.

Ranks: looked up from real players' current ranks, refreshed weekly. How close they land to real ranks is in the self-check below. "More pp than" is the same number as a share of the leaderboard. Entering your own rank shows it in place of the estimate. Future ranks assume today's leaderboard; in practice everyone else keeps climbing too.

Overwatch equivalent: your share of active osu! players (10+ hours, played recently; about a third of the sample; past 6,000 pp, from real ranks) you have more pp than, matched to the same spot on Overwatch's rank ladder. Blizzard hasn't published a breakdown since adding Emerald (Aug 2026), so the shares are rebuilt from its last chart (July 2025) the way the Emerald patch notes describe the change: Emerald took old high Platinum and old low Diamond, and every other rank shifted half a tier. That gives Bronze 1.2%, Silver 7.5%, Gold 22.2%, Platinum 33.4%, Emerald 24.8%, Diamond 9.1%, Master 1.7%, Grandmaster 0.15%, Champion 0.025%, and puts the 50% mark at mid Platinum, which matches the in-game ranking screen. Divisions are split evenly within each tier, and the two ladders are different populations. The filter is there because Overwatch's ladder, like most ranked games, only counts people who unlocked ranked and played it that season, while osu!'s leaderboard keeps everyone who played in roughly the last year, including many who barely played or have stopped. Filtering to active players makes Overwatch ranks about a division lower than counting every 10+ hour account.

Accuracy: your Hit Accuracy against players at about your pp, from the same dumps (the dumps' accuracy matches the profile's Hit Accuracy). Accuracy rises fast with pp, so only players very close to your pp are compared, and the comparison is rebuilt monthly.

Star level: the median star rating of the top 10 plays (without DT, HR, EZ, HT or FL, which change a map's stars) for thousands of real players from the osu! API, fitted as a curve in log space. Players at the same pp typically spread about ±8% around it. DT players' level reads low because their DT plays are left out. Checked on players left out of the fit (Oct 2026), the curve was within 0.15★ of the real median in every pp bracket.

Projection: from thousands of players' histories (dated top-100 scores and plays per month, rebuilt into pp and hours over time; players who mostly play other modes left out), compared 12 months apart. Rebuilding past pp from today's top 100 has a blind spot: scores a player later beat or pushed out of their top 100 are gone. Every reasonable way of handling that agrees players drift back toward the trend; they put the speed between 0.3 and 0.6, which is why the range is wide.

Data: data.ppy.sh random_10000 dumps and the osu! API; players with under 1 hour logged are left out of the curve. Hours = total_seconds_played. Past about 1,000 hours only 2% of players remain, so read that region loosely. The curve rebuilds itself monthly, ranks weekly and the projection monthly; this version was built recently.
